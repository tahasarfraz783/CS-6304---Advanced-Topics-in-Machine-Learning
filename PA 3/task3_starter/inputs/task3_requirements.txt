Introduction and Terminology
Test-time compute is computation spent answering a query while model parameters remain fixed. Additional
computation can produce more candidate solutions, develop an existing solution further, or evaluate alternatives before
choosing an answer. The question is how effectively this computation is used, rather than simply how many tokens are
generated.
Parallel sampling generates independent reasoning trajectories, or rollouts, for the same query. Best-of-N
selection returns the highest-scoring candidate, whereas self-consistency aggregates candidates by their final
answers. Repeated sampling can increase the chance of finding a correct answer, but a selection rule must recognize
it. Oracle coverage measures whether any candidate is correct; it is an analysis upper bound that uses reference
answers, not a deployable selection method.
10
Sequential refinement conditions a new response on an earlier solution and a request to review it. It spends
computation on reconsideration rather than independent exploration. Search methods combine branching with
intermediate evaluation; an outcome verifier evaluates completed solutions, while a process verifier evaluates
intermediate steps. Here, model confidence and visual engagement provide internal quality signals without an additional
verifier model.
Self-certainty summarizes the model’s full next-token distributions, while sampled-token log-probability measures
support for the tokens actually generated. For VLMs, visual engagement measures the share of attention allocated
to the image. VisConf combines visual engagement with candidate-calibrated self-certainty. Rank-weighted
consensus (RWC) combines rollout quality with agreement by assigning larger votes to higher-ranked rollouts.
Adaptive sampling varies the number of rollouts according to the evidence observed so far. A stopping policy trades
the cost of another sample against its potential to change or improve the answer. This task investigates these choices
using supplied LLM/VLM rollouts, a small live refinement experiment, and simulated adaptive inference.

• Quality-aware: require the same agreement condition, plus agreement between the self-consistency and RWC

Define the RWC margin as the difference between the largest and second-largest answer votes divided by total vote

What to Watch For
• Answer equivalence affects both voting and grading. Missing extractions and capped outputs remain part of the
experiment; do not remove them to improve scores.
• Gold answers and future candidates are available for retrospective analysis, not for selection, stopping, or candidaterelative normalization.
• Consensus, self-certainty, and visual attention are evidence signals, not guarantees of correctness. Token-level
associations do not establish causal visual grounding.
• Compare live strategies using their actual input/output costs and runtime as well as output allowances. Three
short responses can encounter different truncation rates from one long response.
• Treat offline token cost as a proxy. Scoring and collecting all-layer attention have additional deployment costs that
are not timed in the supplied export.
• Bootstrap questions, not individual rollouts. Results compare two model–dataset combinations; they do not isolate
the causal effect of adding vision.
Required Evidence
• Sampling and selection: accuracy and oracle-coverage curves for both datasets, a compact method comparison,
and the decomposition of sampling versus selection failures.
• Signal analysis: length and correctness associations, confidence–vision relationships, and the tokenwise/trajectorylevel fusion comparison.
• Live inference: accuracy, actual input/output tokens, runtime, truncation/extraction rates, and revision
transitions for the three strategies on the 50 questions.
• Adaptive inference: development-selected thresholds, held-out accuracy–cost results, stopping distributions, and
comparisons with fixed budgets.
• Qualitative findings: a small set of aligned examples illustrating the observed selection, refinement, and stopping
behavior.

Research Questions
1. Exploration and selection. What limits the benefit of repeated sampling, and how does the balance between
candidate coverage and selection quality change with compute?
2. Evidence and consensus. How do confidence, visual engagement, and answer agreement contribute to selecting
a final answer, and what explains their successes or failures?
3. Breadth and refinement. How does spending computation on independent alternatives compare with a longer
response or sequential reconsideration?
4. Adaptive allocation. When is another rollout useful, and how effectively can the observed evidence guide
stopping without access to correctness?
5. Compute and reliability. Which strategies achieve useful accuracy–cost trade-offs, and what do the observed
failures reveal about their reliability and limitations?
